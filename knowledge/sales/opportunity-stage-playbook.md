# Salesforce Opportunity Stage Playbook

Status: SME-review template. This is not historical customer evidence.

## Discovery

Confirm the business problem, measurable impact, urgency, current process, decision criteria,
decision process, budget owner, technical stakeholders, and target date. Do not advance the
opportunity merely because a meeting occurred. Record gaps explicitly.

Recommended plays:

1. Account Executive, within two business days: document the customer's problem and desired outcome.
2. Account Executive, within three business days: identify an economic buyer and operational champion.
3. Solutions Consultant, before stage exit: validate technical requirements and integration constraints.
4. Seller, before stage exit: agree a mutual next step and date with the customer.

Exit evidence: confirmed problem, quantified impact, named stakeholders, decision process, and agreed next step.

## Solution Validation

Map capabilities to confirmed requirements. Demonstrations should use the customer's priority workflows.
Capture unresolved security, architecture, implementation, legal, and data questions.

Recommended plays:

1. Solutions Consultant: conduct a tailored demonstration or workshop.
2. Account Executive: validate success criteria and obtain customer confirmation.
3. Implementation lead: provide a realistic onboarding plan for complex deals.
4. Security owner: start security review early when regulated or sensitive data is involved.

Exit evidence: solution fit confirmed, risks owned, implementation path understood, and buying group aligned.

## Proposal and Negotiation

Review commercial scope against the validated solution and outcomes. Do not offer unapproved discounts or
commitments. Confirm procurement, legal, security, and signature processes before relying on a close date.

Recommended plays:

1. Account Executive: present value, scope, assumptions, and commercial terms.
2. Sales Manager: review discount and deal-risk requests.
3. Account Executive: create a mutual action plan with customer owners and dates.
4. Legal or Deal Desk: handle nonstandard terms through approved processes.

Exit evidence: verbal alignment, procurement and legal path, authorized signer, and dated close plan.

## Closed Won and Closed Lost

For won deals, capture the decisive value, successful plays, objections resolved, timeline, and expansion signals.
For lost deals, capture the primary and contributing loss reasons, competitor, missing capability, timing, price,
stakeholder gaps, and whether nurture is appropriate. Use these records to improve future recommendations.
